01 / Computer vision & digital health

Vision-language models for digital pathology.

I study few-shot whole-slide classification using a pretrained computational pathology vision-language model and attention-based multiple instance learning. The experiments compare visual adapters with textual task residuals.

For predicting ethanol drinking status from nonhuman primate liver histology, the eight-dimensional visual adapter achieved 78.1% mean balanced accuracy in the four-shot setting, compared with 43.5% for the ABMIL baseline. Evaluation used subject-disjoint splits and 30 experimental runs.

Attention heatmaps connect slide-level predictions to image regions and support interpretation alongside pathologist review.

02 / Foundation-model adaptation

Distribution-aware adaptation for classification.

I developed histogram-based parameter-efficient tuning to adapt pretrained Transformers to new classification tasks. A trainable histogram module captures feature distributions alongside frozen Transformer components.

In passive and active sonar classification experiments, I compared adaptation strategies through prediction accuracy, trainable parameters, and learned representations. On VTUAD, histogram-based tuning achieved 91.8% accuracy, compared with 89.8% for the conventional adapter baseline.

03 / Physiological time series

Physics-informed models for physiological prediction.

I developed physics-informed neural-network models as part of a collaborative study of cuffless blood-pressure estimation. The approach combines supervised learning with physiological relationships expressed through Taylor approximations.

The published study reduced ground-truth training requirements by an average factor of 15 relative to the tested time-series baselines, while maintaining strong blood-pressure prediction performance.

04 / Biomedical sensing

An integrated wearable for stress detection.

During my master’s research, I designed an ECG/EDA wearable system, developing its circuitry, Bluetooth Low Energy electronics, and supervised stress-detection algorithms.

The project achieved 94% classification accuracy in an 18-participant study and threefold battery life relative to the comparison devices.
