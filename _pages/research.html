Evaluating parameter-efficient vision-language modeling for few-shot computational pathology.

This proof-of-concept study evaluates parameter-efficient adaptation of CONCH, a pretrained pathology vision-language model, to predict ethanol drinking status from rhesus macaque liver whole-slide images. The pipeline, built upon the MIL-Adapter framework, combines attention-based multiple instance learning with visual adapters or learned residuals in the text embeddings while keeping the image and text encoders frozen. Using 37 slides and four training subjects per class, a visual adapter achieved 78.1% mean balanced accuracy, compared with 43.5% for the baseline without adaptation. Evaluation used subject-disjoint training, validation, and test sets over 30 runs.

Distribution-guided parameter-efficient tuning for waveform-based sensing.

This study introduces histogram-based parameter-efficient tuning (HPT), which adapts pretrained Transformers by learning distributions of intermediate features while keeping the backbone frozen. Histogram layers add target-domain statistics alongside frozen self-attention components, helping the model accommodate differences between pretraining and downstream data. Evaluation covers three passive-sonar datasets and two active-sonar image datasets. On VTUAD, HPT achieved 91.8% mean classification accuracy with 153,000 trainable parameters, compared with 89.8% for the best tested conventional adapter. The method also remained competitive with other parameter-efficient approaches on active sonar imagery.
