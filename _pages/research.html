01 / Computer vision & digital health

Evaluating vision-language models for few-shot digital pathology.

This proof-of-concept study evaluates parameter-efficient adaptation of CONCH, a pretrained pathology vision-language model, to predict ethanol drinking status from rhesus macaque liver whole-slide images. The pipeline combines attention-based multiple instance learning with visual adapters or learned residuals in the text embeddings while keeping the image and text encoders frozen. Using 37 slides and four training subjects per class, a visual adapter achieved 78.1% mean balanced accuracy, compared with 43.5% for the baseline without adaptation. Evaluation used subject-disjoint training, validation, and test sets over 30 runs.

02 / Acoustic representation & fusion

Dual-encoder fusion for underwater acoustic classification.

This framework combines pretrained waveform and spectrogram encoders to classify underwater vessel sounds using complementary temporal and spectral information. Small fine-tuning modules adapt each frozen backbone, while a differentiable Choquet integral fuses their class probabilities and learns how each representation contributes to individual vessel classes. Experiments on DeepShip and ShipsEar use recording-level data splits to compare the combined model with single-encoder baselines. The results show that jointly using both representations can improve classification while keeping the number of trainable parameters small. Learned fusion weights also reveal class-specific preferences for waveform and spectrogram information.

03 / Foundation-model adaptation

Distribution-aware tuning for sonar classification.

This study introduces histogram-based parameter-efficient tuning (HPT), which adapts pretrained Transformers by learning distributions of intermediate features while keeping the backbone frozen. Small histogram modules add target-domain statistics alongside frozen self-attention components, helping the model accommodate differences between pretraining and downstream data. Evaluation covers three passive-sonar datasets and two active-sonar image datasets. On VTUAD, HPT achieved 91.8% mean classification accuracy with 153,000 trainable parameters, compared with 89.8% for the best tested conventional adapter. The method also remained competitive with other parameter-efficient approaches on active sonar imagery.

04 / Biomedical sensing

Integrated physiological sensing for mental stress detection.

This study integrates low-power electrocardiogram (ECG) and electrodermal activity (EDA) sensing with signal processing and supervised learning to distinguish mental stress from relaxation. Analysis of a public physiological dataset guided the choice of two signals, reducing sensor requirements while preserving classification performance. A custom sensor board collects both signals and transmits them over Bluetooth Low Energy. In a controlled study of 18 participants completing mental arithmetic and Stroop tasks, the system achieved 94.4% mean classification accuracy with a k-nearest-neighbor classifier. The prototype consumed less than 16 mW and supported up to 70 hours of battery operation during transmission.
